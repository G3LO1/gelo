# Roblox genre notes

Snapshot of genre patterns up to mid-2026. Trends move fast: verify current charts before relying on saturation calls. Example games show what the bar looks like, they are not a full list.

## Platform facts that shape every idea
- Most sessions are on phones; touch controls, small screens and low-end devices are the default. PC-only mechanics (precise aim, many keybinds) shrink the audience.
- The player base is wide: a large under-13 audience plus a majority of 13+ players, growing fastest in older teens and young adults. Pick who the game is for; "everyone" usually means nobody.
- Discovery is driven by engagement and monetization metrics measured on real players. A new game gets a test window; if retention and playtime are weak, recommendations dry up regardless of idea quality.
- Players arrive with friends and join friends' servers. Games that are better with friends spread on their own.
- Live-ops is expected: hit games update weekly or bi-weekly with events, limited items, codes. A game with no update plan decays within weeks.
- Earning: game passes, developer products, Premium payouts based on Premium members' playtime, subscriptions, rewarded video ads, UGC/avatar items. Paid random items must show odds and are restricted in some countries.
- Experiences need a maturity/content questionnaire; 17+ content requires age-verified players and cuts the audience.
- YouTube/TikTok creators drive most viral hits: ideas that produce funny, dramatic or shareable moments get free marketing.

## Patterns that keep winning
- A simple verb repeated with escalating rewards (collect, grow, steal, survive, upgrade).
- Progress that continues while the player is away, plus a reason to check in daily.
- Visible status: rare items, pets, outfits, leaderboards others can see.
- Player-to-player interaction that creates stories: trading, stealing, protecting, betraying, co-op panic.
- Timed scarcity: rotating shops, limited events, weekend boosts.
- Thumbnail-friendly concept: one striking image explains the game.

## Patterns that usually fail
- "Like game X but better graphics": graphics rarely beat an incumbent's content and community.
- Huge scope before proving the loop (open worlds, MMOs, story campaigns).
- Mechanics that only work with full servers.
- Long tutorials before the first fun moment.
- Pay-to-win as the main income with no free path: players leave and leave bad votes.
- Riding a trend that already peaked weeks ago with no twist.
